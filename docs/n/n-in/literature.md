# In-Channel Roughness (n-in)

While a single lumped roughness value is convenient for simple 1D routing, advanced 2D hydraulic modeling requires differentiating the main channel from the floodplain. **n-in** represents the roughness governing flow *below* the bankfull depth.

## Drivers of In-Channel Resistance
In-channel resistance is dominated by:
1. **Bed Material Resistance:** Skin friction generated by the predominant grain sizes (gravel, cobble, sand, silt).
2. **Basin driange chartersitcs:** Watershed scale attributes such as catchment slope, drainage density, and basin shape dictate the hydrological regime and sediment delivery rates. These factors control peak discharge, flood wave dynamics, and sediment supply/caliber, which in turn dictate the relative submergence and energy slope of the channel. 

## Chow's Compound Channel Method
To parameterize n-in from historical data, we apply Chow's compound channel method. This analytical technique breaks the observed rating curve precisely at the bankfull depth. By doing so, the hydraulic influence of the floodplain is stripped away, allowing us to isolate and optimize the true in-channel resistance coefficient independently.

Isolating n-in is critical because combining in-channel and overbank conveyance into a single parameter often leads to systematic underestimation of in-channel velocities and overestimation of in-channel stages during non-flood conditions.
